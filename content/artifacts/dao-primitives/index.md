The DAO Primitives Framework provides a systematic approach to designing and implementing decentralized organizations as purpose-aligned networks. Building on the need for organizational models that can harness collective intelligence while adapting to complexity, this framework offers conceptual tools and practical patterns for building effective decentralized systems.

## Framework Overview

Traditional organizational models, optimized for industrial production and hierarchical management, struggle with complexity, adaptability, and equitable value distribution. Decentralized organizations offer transformative alternatives through distributed authority, permissionless innovation, transparent operations, and aligned incentives.

The DAO Primitives Framework addresses a crucial challenge: **how can decentralized networks scale while balancing efficiency with innovation, autonomy with alignment, and flexibility with coherence?**

## Core Components

### Dimensional Lenses

Three key perspectives for understanding organizational contexts:

- **Group Phase**: Five lifecycle stages from Conversation through Formation, Organization, Coordination, to Completion
- **Group Scale**: Four operational levels - Collaboration (sub-10), Coordination (10-150), Constituency (100s+), and Network scale
- **Group State**: Transparent documentation of Purpose, Practice, and Progress

### Organizational Primitives

Fundamental building blocks that can be combined to create tailored organizational systems:

- **DAOs**: Purpose-aligned networks of small autonomous teams
- **Cells**: Teams of fewer than 10 people collaborating on specific ongoing activities
- **Roles**: Sets of responsibilities, permissions, and accountabilities
- **Tasks**: Defined pieces of work with clear deliverables

## Framework Applications

The framework serves multiple contexts:

- **New Organization Design**: Structured approach with templates and guidance for appropriate patterns
- **Existing Organization Evolution**: Diagnostic tools and incremental pathways for distributed transformation
- **Inter-organizational Coordination**: Shared language, interoperability standards, and network governance patterns

## Learning from Practice

The framework is informed by real-world implementation through case studies including All In For Sport, Institute for Community Sustainability, and the Reimagining Power Project's multi-stakeholder governance structure.
